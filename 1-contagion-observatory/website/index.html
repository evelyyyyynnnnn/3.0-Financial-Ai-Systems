Tail lift is how much more often the destination crashes on days the source crashes than on other days, averaged over the pairs with no direct link. Roughly 43% of the apparent co-crashing disappears once the common market factor is removed. That is the headline: most of what looks like contagion in raw return data is the market moving as one, and what is left over is the part a transmission model is obliged to account for.

Why the accuracy figures are not on this page

Edge recovery needs a graph to score against

the real transmission graph is unknown -- that is the question, not the answer -- so edge-recovery precision has no denominator

Correlation is everywhere in market data and almost none of it is transmission, so the method is validated first on a demo market where the transmitting edges are planted and therefore known. Precision, recall and shock propagation are measured there and reported there, and are never carried across to this run.

Reproduce

python -m src.demo --real   # the real Ken French panel
python -m src.demo          # the synthetic panel, with planted known edges
# -> results/latest-real.json
pytest -q
