What changed since the last filing

Companies restate their risk factors every year, and the edits are where the news is. This compares each filing's Item 1A and Item 7 against the prior period and reports three things: disclosures that are new, ones that were dropped, and — the ones worth reading first — ones that were kept and quietly escalated with language the earlier version did not carry.

How a finding is produced. The filing's HTML is flattened to text, cut into Items on the Regulation S-K headings, and split into sentences. Each current sentence is matched against the prior filing by containment — how much of an earlier sentence still survives inside it — rather than symmetric similarity, so a disclosure that was kept word-for-word and then extended is recognised as the same disclosure rather than a new one. A match whose new wording introduces risk language the prior version lacked is an escalation.

Every finding quotes its source. Nothing here is paraphrased or model-generated: the text shown is the sentence as filed, and escalations show the prior sentence beside it so the change is checkable without opening the filing.
